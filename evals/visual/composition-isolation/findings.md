# Composition Isolation Findings

## Test scope

Four editorial posters used the same fictional event, exact seven-string text inventory, subject origin, function, object family, morphology, assembly, treatment, scale role, focal event, palette, topology, and type mode. Only the Composition Contract changed. Each case used one initial generation and no retry. The four selected proofs are all `1080 × 1440` opaque PNGs.

## Observed evidence shared by two or more tests

- All four posters kept `VIBE CODING WORKSHOP` as the first reading event at full size and at approximately 25% thumbnail size.
- All four retained one coherent bubble-capsule subject mass with the same recognizable anchors: oversized convex canopy, rounded shell, curved connection seam, and warm upholstered interior.
- All four used a continuous `EDGE_BLOOM` background across the four corners and full perimeter; none introduced transparency, a missing canvas region, or a floating UI card.
- All four showed the seven locked designed strings once each, with no visible extra poster copy. The support elements stayed subordinate to the title.
- All four kept one visibly quieter release region. The release region changed location and shape with the layout rather than disappearing into generic background space.
- All four attached support copy to an explicit axis, baseline, curve, or diagonal rule. No case showed metadata floating independently from the main structure.
- The same morphology remained warm, rounded, and function-bearing in every case. No case collapsed into an obvious present-day EV, appliance, or phone.

## Layout-specific behavior

### `ASYMMETRIC_COUNTERWEIGHT`

Observed: The capsule owns the upper-right and is cropped by the edge; the title and support system occupy the lower-left and connect through one curved rule. The broad counter-space is immediately legible at thumbnail size.

Interpretation: This was the clearest case of unequal spatial masses. It avoids a safe left-title/right-object split because the object crop, shared curve, and counter-space relationship operate as one structure.

### `CENTERED_ANCHOR`

Observed: The capsule is centered on a long horizontal baseline beneath the title. The title remains left-weighted and substantially larger than the centered object.

Interpretation: The baseline makes the anchor contract visible and keeps the subject from floating. The main risk is generic centered-template symmetry; this proof avoids that risk through an offset chromatic field and non-symmetric title block, but the risk remains a layout-specific watch item.

### `PERSPECTIVE_VECTOR`

Observed: The title, deck, support lines, and capsule all follow a strong lower-left to upper-right diagonal. The upper-right field functions as the quiet release region.

Interpretation: The diagonal is structural rather than ornamental because it carries both copy and subject. Support text is the least readable at thumbnail scale, suggesting that vector alignment can spend more of the support readability budget than the other contracts.

### `TYPE_DOMINANT_FIELD`

Observed: The title occupies most of the left field in three oversized lines. The capsule is small, lower-right, and clearly subordinate; the right side remains a quiet counter-field.

Interpretation: This gives the strongest title-first hierarchy and the most obvious scale anomaly. It also reduces the subject's functional contribution to the overall read, so the contract is a stronger choice when the message must be typographic rather than object-led.

## Generator variance versus contract effect

- Contract effect: object placement, title footprint, relationship axis, release-zone location, and support-copy attachment changed in repeatable ways across the four outputs.
- Generator variance: typeface weight, exact line breaks, surface lighting, cloud/haze placement, and the capsule's small hardware details varied even though the selected recipe fields were fixed.
- The repeated hierarchy result is more likely contract-driven than generator-driven because all four independent outputs preserve title-first reading while their spatial relationships differ in the predicted directions.
- The weaker support legibility on `PERSPECTIVE_VECTOR` is an observed case-specific signal, not yet a grammar rule. It needs another controlled comparison before being treated as a repeated failure class.

## Hypotheses for the integration owner

- Hypothesis: `CENTERED_ANCHOR` needs an explicit anti-template check around axial symmetry when the title and object are both visually simple.
- Hypothesis: `PERSPECTIVE_VECTOR` may need a support-copy readability guard when the vector angle becomes steep; this is not repeated evidence yet.
- Hypothesis: `TYPE_DOMINANT_FIELD` should be preferred when title dominance is the primary objective, but may under-communicate a functional speculative subject unless the object remains large enough to retain its identity anchors.

No exact wording changes to `SKILL.md` or the visual catalogs are proposed from this single four-case review.
