# IMAGE_MASK_FIELD — Photo Extraction Findings

## Question

Does a supplied photo work better when the skill extracts the important elements and recomposes them into the poster, while preserving the skill's gradient, palette, and editorial atmosphere?

## Observed evidence

- The improved proof does not use the source as a full rectangular photograph. It extracts the main woman as a recognizable halftone/duotone figure and preserves large palm fronds as selected green graphic silhouettes.
- The woman's face, dark hair, white floral dress, pose, and body silhouette remain recognizable. The dense green foliage remains a primary visual element rather than disappearing into a background crop.
- The title `DIGITAL MEDIA GALLERY` shares the extracted image system through color separation, halftone texture, overprint, and contour relationships. It is not placed inside a plain card or pasted over an untouched photo.
- The `IMAGE_MASK_FIELD` negative-space cut remains visible, but it behaves as a shaped release zone inside a gradient composition rather than as a white overlay panel.
- The warm ivory / pale pistachio / apricot gradient continues behind and between the extracted elements and covers the full canvas.
- At approximately 25% scale, title-first hierarchy, person recognition, and leaf rhythm all survive.

## Comparison with the previous proof

The previous proof preserved the photo well but treated it mostly as a rectangular image field with a pale title mask. That made the title feel overlaid and left the source photo responsible for most of the composition. The improved proof makes the person and foliage the actual subject mass and lets typography participate in the same printed image layers.

## Interpretation

The evidence supports an extraction-first workflow for supplied images:

1. identify 2–4 identity anchors;
2. separate the important subject elements from nonessential background;
3. recombine those elements with the selected composition contract;
4. integrate title and image through shared texture, color plate, edge, contour, or overprint;
5. restore the skill's continuous gradient as the bottommost atmospheric field.

This is a stronger design direction, not yet a reliability claim. It was tested on one photo and one successful proof.

## Risks and follow-up

- Portrait photos, scenes, and low-contrast images may require different extraction strategies.
- More aggressive title/image fusion can damage glyph readability or cover a face; identity and text-clear-zone checks must remain hard constraints.
- Supplied photographs may contain authentic signs or incidental people. They need an explicit intrinsic-text and nonessential-detail policy.
- Reliability should be tested with multiple source photos and repeated generations before these rules are centralized in `SKILL.md`.

No files outside the evidence lane were changed. The exact skill wording remains for the integration owner to decide.
