# Concept Contract

Use this contract to connect the approved message to observable image behavior before choosing a subject morphology. It prevents attractive but interchangeable imagery without turning future-object design into a closed catalog.

## Required fields

Write one `concept_contract` for every output:

```json
{
  "message_anchor": "the specific claim or action the image must communicate",
  "subject_behavior": "what the subject visibly does or how its state changes",
  "visible_evidence": [
    "one observable cue",
    "an optional second or third cue"
  ],
  "semantic_direction": "the intended progression, relation, or state",
  "must_not_imply": [
    "a contradictory reading",
    "an overly generic fallback reading"
  ]
}
```

Keep every field content-derived and morphology-open. `subject_behavior` describes a visible action, transformation, relation, or state—not a required catalog shape. `visible_evidence` contains one to three cues that can be inspected in the rendered pixels. `must_not_imply` contains one to four likely contradictions or generic substitutions.

## Evidence test

Complete this privately before generation:

> This subject communicates this message because the viewer can see ___ causing, becoming, connecting, repeating, resolving, or changing ___ through ___.

Reject the concept when the explanation relies only on `future`, `guidance`, `progress`, `possibility`, atmosphere, or style. Those words may describe a mood but do not constitute visible evidence.

The evidence must remain understandable from the subject and background without depending on the subtitle. The title may name the idea; the image must provide a concrete visual reason to believe it.

## Subject-origin behavior

- `ABSTRACT_FORM`: express the message through a visible transformation, interval, repetition, collision, closure, or state change. A beautiful isolated form with no content-specific relation fails.
- `ARCHIVE_ARTIFACT`: preserve the real object while using its screen state, orientation, sequence, or surrounding relation as evidence. Reject indicators whose direction contradicts the message, such as descending bars for growth.
- `SPECULATIVE_OBJECT`: expose enough of the hypothetical operation to infer what the object receives, changes, connects, measures, releases, or completes. Purpose-bearing parts support this operation; they are not decorative controls.
- supplied image: preserve identity anchors and derive the concept behavior from crop, repetition, masking, juxtaposition, or environment. Do not invent a false action by distorting the person or source object.

## User-locked subjects

An explicitly requested object has priority. Do not replace it merely because another metaphor would be easier. Strengthen the requested object through a content-specific visible behavior.

For example, if the user locks a lighthouse for `feedback becomes form`, do not settle for “the lighthouse means guidance.” Show several distinct incoming signals entering its optical chamber and leaving as one materially changed beam or projected structure. The user keeps the lighthouse; the image gains causal evidence.

## Direction and contradiction gate

Before generation, name the expected direction or relation in plain language. It may be cyclical, convergent, expanding, completing, balancing, exchanging, pausing, or another content-derived behavior; this is not a finite vocabulary.

Reject the plan when a major visible indicator suggests the opposite direction. After generation, reject the artwork when:

- required evidence is absent or only explained by the copy;
- the dominant motion or data signal contradicts `semantic_direction`;
- a speculative object's operation remains functionally ambiguous;
- a generic beacon, portal, orbit, planet, glow, or decorative form could illustrate several unrelated headlines equally well.

Concept fidelity is structural. Prefer `REGENERATE` over a local edit unless one isolated indicator can be safely corrected without changing text, hierarchy, subject identity, or composition.
